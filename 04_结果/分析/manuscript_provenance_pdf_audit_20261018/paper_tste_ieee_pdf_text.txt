under Degraded Wind-Turbine SCADA Telemetry

Abstract—Pre-dispatch operating reserve screening near the
wind-turbine aerodynamic boundary is governed by asymmetric
shortfall risk. We study how the minimum suﬀicient model
changes when SCADA telemetry is delayed or blade pitch is
unavailable, separating state recoverability from reserve-decision
value. On the 134-turbine WTB benchmark, deterministic aero-
dynamic rules achieve the lowest evaluated boundary-band sur-
rogate cost under fresh telemetry (589,535 kW ⋅h at 10 min;
881,367 kW ⋅h at 60 min). Under synthetic 10–30 min delays,
non-neural state-conditional recalibration absorbs 36.3%–61.7%
of within-condition shortage loss; absorption reaches 66.5% at the
60-min stress endpoint. With pitch withheld at deployment but
available during historical training, active-power consequences
retain boundary information (NMI = 0.461), while representation
metrics are not interpreted as decision-value evidence. The latest
imported strong-baseline snapshot gives posterior conditioning a
+445,639 kW⋅h full-population penalty relative to site-local wind-
speed quantiles; this candidate remains pending deep audit. The
diagnostics motivate comparisons of physical rules, recalibration
and direct local quantiles by information regime; they do not
certify model suﬀiciency or universal neural superiority. External
transfer evidence is limited to a deterministic bounded branch
and does not establish universal cross-site value.
Index Terms—Wind power reserve, SCADA telemetry degrada-
tion, latent operating boundary, state-conditional recalibration,
minimum suﬀicient model, consequence-based recovery.
I. INTRODUCTION
In bulk power system operations, real-time generation short-
falls incur severe asymmetric shortage penalties relative to
surplus headroom; conventional symmetric metrics like root-
mean-square error (RMSE) mask severe tail shortfall events
that govern operating reserve adequacy in Level-1 pre-dispatch
screening [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]. This operational vulnerabili-
ty concentrates near the aerodynamic demarcation boundary
between Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT, Region 2)
and active blade-pitch regulation (Region 3). Near rated inflow
velocity, local power sensitivity transitions abruptly from a
steep cubic trajectory (𝜕𝑃/𝜕𝑣∝𝑣2) toward zero as pitch
actuators shed aerodynamic lift [6], [7]. When operating states
shift across this threshold unannounced, deterministic power
curves over-extrapolate generation into Region 3, precipitating
severe unhedged reserve deficits.
This vulnerability is exacerbated by telemetry degradation in
commercial Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCA-
DA) systems, including gateway serialization overhead, packet
queuing backlogs, and intermittent cellular transmission jitter
[8], [9]. In operational archives (such as Kelmarsh), 99.6%
of discrete turbine status transitions occur between standard
10-minute polling boundaries. Furthermore, in third-party ag-
gregator and Virtual Power Plant (VPP) triage, blade-pitch
availability is treated here as an imposed evaluation condition;
its commercial prevalence is not established. When delayed
or missing measurements enter physical power equations, dis-
patch systems clear reserve margins against obsolete operating
points. With pitch withheld, the control register is unavailable,
but the evaluated valid regime labels remain separable by
fresh wind speed; consequence-signal classification therefore
tests redundant information rather than necessary latent-state
recovery (𝑍𝑡→𝑃𝑡, 𝑄𝑡, trajectory).
Existing literature frequently treats aerodynamic curves and
deep neural networks as competing predictive paradigms [10],
[11], [12], [13], [14], [15], [16], overlooking their distinct
operational boundaries under information freshness constraints.
Rather than asking which model achieves marginal predictive
gains, bulk power system operations require answering a
foundational engineering question:

II. RELATED WORK
A. From Average Wind-Power Accuracy to Transition-Window Risk
Spatio-temporal graph neural networks (STGNNs) advance
multi-step wind plant forecasting by modeling spatial cross-
correlations and wake advection [10], [11], [12], [13], with
physics-informed extensions incorporating wake and bound-
ary constraints [14], [15], [16]. While these architectures
reduce plant-wide 𝐿2 error, continuous trajectory minimization
obscures discrete turbine operating state transitions between
MPPT (Region 2) and pitch regulation (Region 3) that govern
spinning reserve adequacy [17], [18], [19].

IV. CASE STUDY CONFIGURATION AND OPERATIONAL CONSTRAINTS
A. Datasets and Preprocessing
Empirical evaluation spans the 134-turbine WTB benchmark
and the ERA5 thermodynamic contrast patch. WTB (KDD
Cup 2022) comprises 134 turbines over 245 days of 10-minute
SCADA telemetry (𝑇= 35,280, 𝑁= 134, 𝐹= 11) [25], with
chronological 180/30/35-day train/val/test splits (𝐻= 36, 𝑃=
24). ERA5 covers three archived months on a 16×16 grid (𝑇=
2208, 𝑁= 256, 8 features) [26] under 1325/441/442 splits.
Multi-year SCADA records from three European commercial
wind plants (ENGIE La Haute Borne, Kelmarsh, Penmanshiel)
provide diverse turbine architectures for cross-farm evaluation.
B. Controlled Telemetry Degradation Protocols and Realism Map-
ping
To evaluate resilience under communication stress, we sim-
ulate three degradation tiers across five seeds (201–205): (1)
Tier 1 (Nominal Polling, 0–10 min, Clean): fresh SCADA on
10-min grid (IEC 61400-25); (2) Tier 2 (Plausible Degraded
Telemetry, 10–30 min, Delay-1 to 3): transmission lags from
substation buffer backlogs [9], characterizing the operational
transition regime; and (3) Tier 3 (Contingency Stress En-
velope, 60 min, Delay-6): a 6-step synthetic lag evaluating
asymptotic physical breakdown [27], [28] as a stress endpoint
rather than an industrial prevalence claim. Perturbations in-
clude additive Gaussian noise (𝜎𝑣= 1.0 m/s, 𝜎𝛽= 2.0∘) and
a two-state Markov burst dropout chain (𝑝𝐺𝐵= 0.08, 𝑝𝐵𝐵=
0.75, 𝑑≤6) [9].

C. Pre-Dispatch Risk Screening Framework and Resampling Hierar-
chy
The declared backbone recipe uses AdamW, validation-RMSE
stopping, clipping and training mixed precision across seeds
201–205; auxiliary probes and quantile heads have distinct
budgets. This recipe does not bind every historical execution
environment or establish evaluation precision. Validation cal-

input and execution binding remains unverified. Uncertainty

boundaries. Five seeds measure initialization variation, not
independent farms or test periods.
V. EMPIRICAL FINDINGS: MINIMUM SUFFICIENT MODELING ACROSS
OBSERVABILITY REGIMES
A. Regime 1: Fresh Observable Telemetry →Deterministic Aerody-
namics and Operational Breakdown Boundaries (RQ1)
Table I summarizes the multi-day replay dispatch benchmark
for 1-hour ahead operational dispatch (ℎ= 6) evaluated on
the boundary-active operating slice (|𝑣phys −10.5| ≤1.0 m/s,
𝑁= 13,883 cell observations across 134 turbines over the 35-
day test split) under nominal clean telemetry. Table II reports
seed-paired hypothesis tests and mechanism ablations across
operational regimes, along with 95% confidence intervals from
a paired daily cluster bootstrap over the 35 observed test days
(1,000 resamples).
The empirical evidence directly establishes the operational suf-
ficiency of deterministic aerodynamics under fresh observable
telemetry. Under pristine telemetry (𝜏= 0), Continuous Phys-
ical Quantile achieves the lowest surrogate reserve screening
cost within the active boundary band (|𝑣phys −10.5| ≤1.0 m/s,
𝑁= 13,883 cell observations) among all evaluated methods
(589,535 ± 77,390 kW ⋅h at immediate dispatch ℎ= 1 and
881,367 ± 95,741 kW ⋅h at 1-hour dispatch ℎ= 6, with
observed ∼6.8% violation). It outperforms both unrouted
dense networks (STGQ-Dense: 991,181 kW ⋅h) and dynam-
ically routed architectures (STGQ-Routed: 959,027 kW ⋅h) by
8.1%–11.1%. As confirmed in Table II, Continuous Physical
Quantile achieves a −77,659 kW ⋅h advantage over neural
representations, with a 95% daily cluster bootstrap confidence
interval (1,000 paired resamples over 35 observed days) strict-
ly excluding zero ([−106,175, −49,143]). When inflow wind
speed and blade pitch angle are fresh and directly observable,
deterministic aerodynamic equations yield the lowest evaluated
surrogate reserve screening cost. Neural networks, lacking hard
physical saturation bounds, introduce unnecessary variance
under nominal telemetry.
Table I exposes the operational reliability boundary of physical
rules when telemetry becomes stale. Transmission delays of
10–30 minutes introduce latency drift between true inflow
and evaluated registers. Under a 60-minute latency contin-
gency stress test (Delay-6), which serves as an asymptotic
reliability breakdown stress endpoint rather than an industrial
prevalence claim, physical equations evaluate stale state tuples
(𝑣𝑡−𝜏, 𝛽𝑡−𝜏). Near rated wind speed, the localized slope
discontinuity (7) amplifies delayed-inflow errors: turbines that
have crossed into active blade-pitch regulation (𝑍𝑡
= 2)
are evaluated with obsolete fine pitch (𝛽𝑡−𝜏≈0∘), over-
extrapolating power output along the steep cubic trajectory (8).
Unadapted physical quantile violation rates surge from 6.81%
to 24.02% ± 1.69% at ℎ= 1 and 12.20% ± 1.25% at ℎ= 6
(1,136,466 kW ⋅h), breaching the nominal 10% Newsvendor
target (𝑞∗= 0.90) and leaving 127.55 MWh of unhedged
generation shortfall across the array.
B. Regime 2: Stale but Observable Telemetry →Non-Neural Recali-
bration Absorbs Drift (RQ2)
When telemetry channels remain observable under transmis-
sion delays, condition-matched state-conditional recalibration
(9) adjusts quantile margins using held-out validation residuals
undergoing the identical delay. As shown in Table III, state-
conditional recalibration absorbs 36.3% of within-condition
shortage loss at 𝜏= 10 min (37.7 →24.0 MWh), 53.9% at
𝜏= 20 min (51.8 →23.9 MWh), and 61.7% at 𝜏= 30 min
(69.1 →26.4 MWh), holding fleet violation at 7.1%–7.5% and
a surrogate reserve cost of 1,228,609 kW⋅h at the ℎ= 1 contin-
gency endpoint. Under full channel observability, recalibrated
physical curves outperform all neural models (STGQ-Routed:
1,274,047 kW ⋅h; STGQ-Modular: 1,283,855 kW ⋅h).
A mismatched calibration matrix across test latencies 𝜏test ∈
[0, 60] min and calibration horizons 𝜏cal ∈[0, 60] min estab-
lishes the operational limits of recalibration under communi-
cation drift. Recalibrating at 𝜏cal = 10 min withstands latency
drift up to 𝜏test = 20 min while maintaining target-satisfying
tail coverage (8.2%–9.1% violation), but breaks down when
staleness surges to 𝜏test = 60 min (13.8% violation). Under
full channel observability, distribution drift does not require
neural representation learning: state-conditional recalibration
recovers 36.3%–66.5% of the observed shortage loss across
the evaluated latency grid (127.6 →42.8 MWh at the 60-min
contingency endpoint, a 66.5% absorption at 9.5% violation)
without updating network weights.

VI. DISCUSSION: MINIMUM SUFFICIENT MODEL COMPLEXITY
The evaluated diagnostics motivate the conditional criteri-
on of Minimum Suﬀicient Model Complexity: when crit-
ical telemetry is unavailable, latent operating states may
remain recoverable from observable consequence signals.
Whether learned representations are necessary for that recov-
ery, whether they change the risk shape, and whether the
changed risk shape improves the downstream decision are three
separate empirical questions. The evidential chain is therefore
recoverability →risk-shape change →decision value, with no
arrow assumed automatically. In particular, NMI/ARI/AUROC
or calibration cannot substitute for matched PSREI evidence.
Given the current strong local baseline, we do not add further
graph, MoE, or hidden-state architectures; any complex rep-
resentation must demonstrate deployment necessity under the
pre-registered risk-constrained selector in E04. The following
comparisons guide further assessment; none supplies deploy-
ment authorization:
1) Fresh and Observable →Physics: Deterministic aerodynam-
ic curves evaluate pristine telemetry (𝜏= 0) with asymptotic
power conservation (𝜕𝑃/𝜕𝑣∝𝑣2 below rated speed, 𝑃≡
𝑃rated above rated speed). Avoiding out-of-distribution neural
variance, they achieve the lowest evaluated surrogate reserve
screening cost within the boundary band (|𝑣phys −10.5| ≤
1.0 m/s, 𝑁= 13,883 events: 589.5k–881.4k kW ⋅h, observed
∼6.8% violation; Tables~I and~III), without establishing a
selector-certified minimum.
2) Stale but Observable →Recalibration: Under observable
operational latency (10–30 min), state-conditional recalibra-
tion absorbs 36.3%–61.7% of within-condition shortage loss
and maintains violation rates below the nominal 10% target
(7.1%–7.5%) without updating neural weights. In the evaluated
latency grid, static recalibration breaks down at the contingen-
cy horizon (𝜏= 60 min, 13.8% violation), which acts as a
synthetic reliability breakdown stress endpoint rather than an
industrial prevalence claim.

VII. LIMITATIONS
The empirical findings are bounded by four operational and
experimental scope conditions:
1) Synthetic Stress: Impairments are evaluated under synthetic
stress (10–30 min backlogs, 60-min latency, Gilbert-Elliott
dropouts); field units may exhibit sensor icing or individual
pitch actions outside supervisory records.
2) Risk Surrogacy: Evaluation operates at Level-1 pre-dispatch
operating reserve screening via the PSREI newsvendor surro-
gate (𝜌= 10), abstracting Level-2 bulk transmission AC-OPF
and balancing settlements.
3) Horizon Scope: Benchmarks use a 35-day continuous test
split across five random seeds (201–205), omitting multi-
annual climatological cycles. Rated wind speeds vary by
model (12.5 m/s on MM92 vs. 14.5 m/s on MM82), requiring
OEM calibration.
4) Deployment Boundaries: EXP-01 v4 is invalidated by
physical-window mismatch; its numerical cost differences and
intervals are withdrawn from current conclusions. Historical
EXP-02 also requires replay because its physical proxy labels
used mismatched normalization statistics. These failures leave
the corresponding decision questions unresolved. Historical
pitch availability during training must remain distinguished
from pitch-never-recorded deployment scenarios.
VIII. CONCLUSION
This study repositions machine learning in reserve screening
from an indiscriminate forecasting tool to a targeted con-
ditional inference mechanism governed by SCADA observ-
ability regimes. Telemetry degradation creates a hierarchy
of suﬀiciency: use the least complex model suﬀicient for
the arriving information regime. When critical telemetry is
unavailable, latent operating states may remain recoverable
from observable consequence signals; whether learned rep-
resentations are necessary for that recovery, and whether
recovered state information improves downstream decisions,
are separate empirical questions. First, under fresh telemetry,
deterministic aerodynamic power curves minimize surrogate
reserve penalties within the boundary band (|𝑣phys −10.5| ≤

decisions; no transition-localized heterogeneity or allocation
advantage is claimed. The bounded deterministic external

weights; it does not close EXP-01 or establish a universal

a required mechanism: increasing routing complexity is not

rules under clean telemetry, recalibration under observable
drift, and consequence information when critical telemetry
is withheld, while the current evidence does not establish a
transition-specific reserve-sizing advantage. Reserve decisions
remain benchmarked against direct observable conditioning.
The current evidence therefore supports stopping complexity
expansion: state recoverability, risk-shape change, and decision
value remain distinct claims. E04 selected no feasible policy;
E07 remains inactive.

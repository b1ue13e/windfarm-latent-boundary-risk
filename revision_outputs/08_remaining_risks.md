## 2. Categorized Inventory of Remaining Risks

### Risk 1: Synthetic Telemetry Degradation vs. Real-World Communication Storms
- **Classification**: `Shallow Verification`
- **Description**: The 10–60 minute latency profiles (Delay-1 through Delay-6) and two-state Markov-Gilbert dropouts are synthetically generated by lagging or masking historical SCADA archives. In actual utility networks, severe cyber-physical storms or optical fiber severed events may induce simultaneous asynchronous clock drift, out-of-order packet arrival, and non-stationary jitter distributions that differ from our discrete delay models.
- **Reviewer Defense / Mitigation**: We explicitly foreground Delay-6 as a "severe contingency outage-envelope stress condition" rather than a routine latency model. The Markov-Gilbert burst evaluation ($p_{GB}=0.08, p_{BB}=0.75$) provides non-uniform packet loss stress testing, and real SCADA timestamp artifacts from Kelmarsh (where 99.6% of pitch transitions occur between 10-min reporting intervals) prove that asynchronous reporting blind spots are a physical reality.

### Risk 2: Bounded Seed Compliance under Frozen Validation Calibration (60% Rate)
- **Classification**: `Minor Robustness Risk`
- **Description**: Under frozen validation calibration without online margin inflation, the fleet-average test violation rate under Delay-6 settles at $10.6\% \pm 1.1\%$. While this represents a dramatic improvement over unadapted physical curves ($24.0\%$), exactly 3 out of 5 random seeds achieve $\le 10.0\%$ violation, while 2 seeds settle at $11.2\%$ and $11.5\%$.
- **Reviewer Defense / Mitigation**: We explicitly reject claims of "unconditional 100% compliance guarantee." In Section IV and V, we report the exact seed compliance breakdown ($3/5$ seeds, $60\%$) and document that achieving guaranteed $\le 10.0\%$ tail compliance across 100% of seeds requires an additional $+5\%$ conservative margin inflation, trading off surplus reserve holding cost for absolute tail security.

### Risk 3: Asymmetric Directionality in Zero-Shot Cross-Farm Generalization
- **Classification**: `Minor Robustness Risk`
- **Description**: Direct zero-shot transfer without local recalibration exhibits significant directional asymmetry: transferring from Kelmarsh (6 MM92 turbines) to Penmanshiel (15 MM82 turbines) achieves NMI $0.752\text{--}0.770$, whereas transferring from Penmanshiel to Kelmarsh collapses to NMI $0.341\text{--}0.505$ (pooled average $0.557$).
- **Reviewer Defense / Mitigation**: Rather than concealing this discrepancy, we explicitly document it as a negative finding that defines an empirical boundary: autonomous zero-shot cross-farm transfer without site recalibration is fundamentally unreliable. We demonstrate that local chronological retraining on the target site restores regime alignment (NMI $>0.94$), confirming that site-specific aerodynamic parameters ($u_{\mathrm{rated}}, p_{\mathrm{th}}$) must be re-calibrated.

### Risk 4: Downstream BESS Model Linearization and Electrochemical Abstraction
- **Classification**: `Minor Robustness Risk`
- **Description**: The downstream BESS rolling dispatch interface employs a linear program with constant round-trip efficiency ($\eta = 0.90$) and state-of-charge boundaries. It abstracts away non-linear solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) degradation, temperature-dependent C-rate derating, and cell balancing transients.
- **Reviewer Defense / Mitigation**: We have completely moved the BESS MPC formulation to the **Supplementary Material**, retaining only a single illustrative paragraph in the main text. We explicitly state in Section II-D and the Cover Letter that the paper does not claim physical validation of electrochemical cell degradation or electricity market financial cashflows, circumscribing the manuscript scope strictly to the upstream Level-1 PSREI screening proxy.
